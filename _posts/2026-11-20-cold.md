It is 4:30 in the afternoon and the sun has already slipped away. A call murmurs on, and I listen the way one listens to rain. I am due at a colleague's house for dinner. But what I want is to melt into my night clothes, burrow under the blankets, watch the series I'm following on Netflix, and order shrimp Thai red curry with sticky rice. There is a small sadness in me, and I can't say where it came from. Maybe the weather. Maybe the feeling of wanting to be loved, or of not being.

November has arrived, and with it the festive season of this part of the world. The streets are strung with lights, and couples drift through them, hand in hand, nuzzling into each other's coats. It's even a Friday. I know how it will go. I'll step out and needle through the cloud of breath and cold, earphones in, music drowning the hubbub, and feel more alone than ever.

That feeling has become something like my identity. When I wander too far from it, I grow uneasy, as if I had left home and landed in a foreign land.

So here is to forcing myself out of this warm apartment, into the chilly night, to walk among crowds that move as though I don't exist, and to learn their customs again.

I don't quite know what I'm saying. Perhaps I am only describing a feeling to myself.
