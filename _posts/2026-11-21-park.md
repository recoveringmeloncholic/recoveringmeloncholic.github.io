I am about to step out for a long walk in Hyde Park. We have a long relationship, this park and I, one of love and discovery. I first came here in 2018, maybe. Jet lag woke me at 4 am, in August, then September, then December, and the months kept getting colder. Still, every morning I would pull on thin shorts, a running t-shirt and a sweatshirt, and go out to run. I learnt to brave the cold, the seemingly deserted lanes, the rising sun, the occasional fellow runner. The air was peaceful and bitterly chilly, and it mapped my airways and lungs as I breathed. It was such a comfort. I think I was lonely at that time without any outlet, buried in work, and the run made me feel alive. On every trip since, going to the park has been my ritual. Until this one. The busy weeks gave me no room for it.

Today morning started with a sad conversation. I ache for her, but I also found myself thinking about the damage we do to people through our actions. How we scar them in the most tender places, and how the scars change them. Maybe they make them unable to do things they once did easily. With my own vulnerability on top of it, I felt I might say something I shouldn't and keep the cycle turning. So I walked away from my phone for a while, to think clearly.

That is the dilemma of trying to help someone you love. And what does help even mean here? Only my view of what might be good for them. I am done trying to do that for others. I can only do what is good for me, and hope everyone else can find their own way to do the same.

I offered to talk, in two minds, but instead of reeling her in by listening to her, I spoke only about myself. Very intentionally. I could hear the wheels turning on her side, and then I could tell she wanted to step away. Which, if you ask me, was the outcome I wanted. I wanted to talk for myself, not to coax her or undo the weight of the earlier conversation that might set her on a path of recovery. The hurt before the healing. The restraint made a point, and I hope it holds.

Later, while getting breakfast, I felt a deep sadness for the spanner I have thrown into her life. Maybe for the first time I regretted meeting her, despite everything. It is like discovering cocaine. It takes your pain away for a moment, until the withdrawal arrives. Then it draws you back in, calling your name. Do I want to be someone's cocaine? 

I need a hug, so I don't feel even worse about myself this week. The fucking pity pot, I know. Instead of letting these tears roll down my cheeks, I need the park to take me in and wrap me in its arms the way it once did, long ago. It will do me far more good tham this fox-hole. I am like a fox holed up in my own smell and warmth, dwelling in it, shaped by it, becoming more of what I already am. With that comes some pity but more self hatred. 

I also thought about what this blog might do to her. I must make sure that never happens. I am considering retiring it from public view and keeping it in a private corner of my laptop. I know I sometimes crave the validation of strangers for thoughts spoken incoherently, but nothing matters more than not hurting anyone further.

I'll think about it a little longer, when emotions aren't crowding me. If I still feel this way tomorrow morning, next week will be the last week this journal is here. Life comes full circle. I did this once, twelve years ago, when it went private, and I think it is time to do it again.

My love, here I come. Let's take a stroll.
